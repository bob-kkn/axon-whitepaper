# Axon Design Validation Summary

> A record of validating the whitepaper's core claims through ten independent prototypes — five validation slices, one group-channel extension of C, three integration slices, and one end-to-end slice that wires both the issuance and payment paths into a single process. For investor and partner review.
> All results are pre-testnet prototype output; confirmation is left to testnet measurement.

## In brief

The Axon whitepaper makes three large claims — that the **issuance economics** of compute supply are safe against fraud, that the **verification** (re-execution adjudication) protecting that issuance is **actually feasible**, and that the **payment and verification plumbing** built on top **withstands attack**. Within the whitepaper these three axes are arguments, but we did not leave them as assertions. Five independent prototypes each realized a claim in code and stress-tested it under adversarial conditions, and all three axes passed a first-pass validation. Three further slices then took the **abstractions** those validations had left behind — an in-memory ledger, an abstract randomness source, an abstract adjudication oracle — and replaced each with a real structure, to ask whether the assumptions still hold on real plumbing, and what each assumption actually costs. What matters is that these prototypes hold each other up: one slice's measurement fills another slice's assumption, and yet another slice confirms that the assumption holds on the real plumbing. Below is an honest summary of what we verified, what held, and what still remains the province of the testnet.

## Stripping out the abstractions — three integration slices and one end-to-end slice

The five validation slices left abstractions in three places: the L1 ledger was an in-memory dictionary (`MockL1`), the sampling randomness was a premise ("unpredictable after commit"), and the re-execution adjudication was a true/false oracle. Three integration slices replaced each with a real structure, and a fourth slice (E2E) removed the last abstraction — that the slices never actually call each other — along both the issuance and the payment path.

|-------|---------------------|--------|------------|--------------|

Only issuance grows the supply, split exactly into supplier share + verification tax. **Six invariants hold over 500 random transaction DAGs with 0 violations** (the generator produces real double-spend conflicts and the ledger confirms exactly one). ## How it all connects — the chain of validation

The nine validation and integration slices pass assumptions and measurements back and forth to form a single chain, and E2E finally wires that chain into one process (C's group extension G stands outside this chain as an extension slice).

- **A assumed two things** — that the detection probability of sampling is `1−(1−f)^k` (the sampling oracle), and that re-execution adjudication is perfect.
- **B1 realized the first assumption.** When we built the plumbing with real Merkle commitments and post-commit public-randomness sampling, the measured detection rate over many epochs reproduced exactly the closed form A had premised. **A's economic conclusion stands not on an abstraction but on the real plumbing.**
- **B2 refuted the second assumption.** In real-LLM re-execution, heterogeneous-runtime adjudication was not perfect (FAR 0.25–0.375). We injected that measured value back into A as **feedback** and re-swept — and even with imperfect adjudication, repetition and accumulation catch it downstream, so the economic defense still held.
- **C realized the payment path (Chapter 6), and B1 the issuance path (Chapter 7)**, showing by invariants that the dispute and slashing rules the whitepaper had deferred to "fixed at the implementation stage" actually protect the honest party.
- **D1, D2, and D3 stripped out the remaining abstractions.** D1 put a real DAG where `MockL1` had stood, showing double-spend prevention and value conservation by invariants; D2 built the randomness B1 had premised as "unbiasable" as a real beacon and quantified **what that premise costs** (last-revealer grinding); and D3 wired B2's measured-FAR adjudication into B1's abstract adjudication slot to confirm the two slices compose correctly.
- **And D3 refined the feedback in turn.** The feedback sweep rolled adjudication failure once per epoch, but the real protocol re-adjudicates each sampled job independently. So the real detection rate is **higher** than the value the feedback used — meaning A's economic conclusion was a conservative estimate that understated detection relative to the real wiring.

- **Finally, E2E actually connected the chain.** D2's beacon drives B1's sampling and that adjudication becomes a real issuance on D1's ledger; C's channel dispute becomes a real settlement on the same ledger — all wired in one process. And connecting it revealed things only visible once connected: the ledger cannot enforce the issuance anchor, and it cannot see share-to-party correspondence either. If the value passed between adjudication and ledger binds neither the amount nor the attribution, it leaks at the boundary even when every slice is individually correct. The former was then closed by the follow-on slice W with verifier-signed attestations, moving anchor enforcement into consensus (inflated issuance 158/200 → 0/200).

In short, B2 measures reality and feeds A, B1 realizes A's sampling mechanism and confirms A, C and B1 guard the plumbing of both paths, D1, D2, and D3 replace the abstractions beneath that plumbing with real structures while presenting the bill for each assumption, and E2E wires all of it into one process to expose the defects that exist only at the boundaries.

## The five most important findings

1. **Security depends on detection, not on the size of the deposit.** The intuition is "grow the deposit and you are safe," but the simulation pointed to a different center of gravity. Issuance is a repeated game, so independent sampling repeats each epoch and the cumulative detection probability of a node that continues forging converges to 1. Thus the expected value stayed negative even with the deposit driven extremely low, and the only point where the defense actually collapsed was when verifiers all colluded and third-party re-checks vanished. **What bears the load of the design is not the collateral but the random assignment of verifiers and the openness of challenge.**

2. **Heterogeneous-runtime adjudication is imperfect, but the economy holds anyway.** Reproducing the same inference across different hardware and runtimes is hard because of floating-point nondeterminism, and a measured false-negative rate existed (worst-case ≈0.375). This refutes the optimistic assumption but is not a catastrophe — a single epoch's adjudication failure is caught in later epochs, the per-trial cumulative detection exceeds 0.997, and once caught in the end, deposit forfeiture overwhelms the gain. **Even with the measured condition injected, the economic defense of the issuance path holds.**

3. **Every time we made an abstraction real, the assumption presented a bill — and both bills were payable.** When we built the sampling randomness the whitepaper had premised as "unbiasable" as a real commit-reveal beacon, the participant who reveals last could choose among a bounded set of outcomes by deciding whether to withhold its own commitment — detection drops from `d` to `d^(2^g)`. In exchange, a withheld commitment is itself detectable and slashable, so that choice carries a price, and the accumulation of the repeated game recovers the loss. There was also a bill in the opposite direction: the fact that the real protocol re-adjudicates **each** sampled job **raises** detection above the coarse approximation the economic simulation used. **Making an assumption real moves some numbers the wrong way and some the right way — we wrote down both.**

4. **We adversarially audited whether the invariants actually verify anything.** The security properties of the ten slices are verified not through a few scenarios but as **invariants** over hundreds to thousands of arbitrary histories, epochs, and transaction DAGs. But "0 violations" is weak evidence on its own — an invariant that catches nothing also reports 0. So we audited each slice's invariant sweep by **deliberately breaking the code** (neutering the validation gate or deleting an individual check) and re-running the same seeds, counting how many seeds each invariant actually fails on. At first several sweeps passed even with the gate removed entirely — because the generator produced only honest inputs and never exercised the validation path. We fixed this at the root by injecting adversarial submissions into each random history (bad signatures, unbalanced states, double-spends, stale-state replays, leaf-count forgery, sample tampering). Now breaking a gate makes invariants fail on tens to hundreds of seeds (channel validator neutered: 135/200; ledger dropping a valid tx: `dag_soundness` 109/200; sampler ignoring randomness: 300/300). We also fixed the real defects an adversarial review caught before merge — a cooperative close replaying a stale state (domain separation), a Merkle tree not binding the leaf count (binding n into the root), a settlement path accepting a negative share (outright rejection), and tautological invariants in PoI proof verification and ledger DAG soundness (replaced with independent recomputation). **That verification passed means it could have failed and did not — and we proved it could have, in seed counts.**

## Honest limits

These ten slices are all **pre-testnet prototypes** and do not substitute for measurement. Specifically:

- **Both paths are now one process — except for group channels.** The E2E slice wires beacon → sampling → adjudication → ledger issuance, and the payment spine wires channel dispute → ledger settlement, so the earlier limit — "the slices never actually call each other" — is resolved **across the whole bilateral path**. What remains is group-channel (G) settlement, and it remains not for want of code but because **the ledger's escrow schema is bilateral** (next item). And wiring end to end is not the same as wiring a network: P2P remains roadmap, and a system with many real nodes running is the province of the testnet.
- **Group-channel settlement was resolved by extending the ledger to N parties (per-party funding and dispute adjudication remain).** The schema limit the payment spine surfaced — `deposit` builds a 2-party escrow and `settle` restricts payout to those two — was closed by the follow-on slice M, which extended the escrow to a sorted N-party set. An N-party group settlement now confirms as-is (2-party is the N=2 special case), and whether a confirmed settlement's recipients are all parties of that channel is verified by a primary-fact invariant that compares against the member set the generator recorded at deposit — inject a real defect into the shared gate and only that invariant fires (99/100) while the other eight stay silent. Two things remain: multi-party funding, where each participant stakes their own share, does not yet exist (a single sender still locks the whole total), and the group dispute adjudication belongs to a layer outside the ledger.
- **Anchor enforcement moved from node policy into consensus, and the single verifier key was extended to a k-of-N committee.** The gap E2E surfaced ("the ledger cannot enforce the issuance anchor") was closed by the follow-on slice (W). Replacing the opaque token with a **verifier-signed attestation** (ed25519 over the amount and issuer, verifier public keys registered at genesis) and deleting the node relay policy (`admissible`) entirely, inflated issuance went 158/200 → 0/200 — and disabling the ledger's signature check reproduces 158/200 exactly, measured proof that enforcement is now inside consensus. A further slice (K) then extended the single registered key to a **k-of-N committee multi-signature**: authorizations carry a signature set `((verifier_id, sig), …)`, and the ledger issues only when at least k **distinct** valid signatures from registered verifiers are present (a duplicate counts once, an unregistered or mismatched signature counts zero, so a k−1 collusion is rejected; all ten constructed attacks are rejected by the ledger; N=1,k=1 reduces to the prior single signature). The pre-merge review returned **SOUND for the first time with no blocking defect**, and the `committee_threshold` invariant is proven non-mirror — lowering the gate to accept one signature fires it 100/100, while the state-reading mirror fires 0/100. Footnote: an empty committee (threshold 0) would allow an unsigned issuance, blocked by clamping the effective threshold to `max(1, k)`.
- **The next slice (S) turned the committee into a draw, and measured that the draw is breakable — that finding is the slice's main result.** Verifiers register via `stake_tx`, locking a uniform bond; each epoch a committee of C is drawn from the whole registry by ranking `sha256(seed || verifier_id)` and taking the lowest C; the ledger counts only those members' valid signatures (registered-but-undrawn contributes zero, and an epoch with no seed rejects issuance). End to end, the same beacon round that drives sampling also draws the committee. **That much is solid**: capture probability matches the hypergeometric tail within 3σ (n=20, C=5, k=3, 20,000 draws); the `committee_membership` invariant re-derives the committee locally from the generator's primary-fact seed, so regressing the gate to registry-wide acceptance fires it 100/100 while the mirror form fires 0/100; and three mutants — a constant beacon output, a widened committee, a truncated draw — each kill tests. **But the ranking input contains a value the adversary chooses for itself — the verifier id — and the seed is public before the fold.** An adversary registering after seeing the seed searches for ids that outrank every honest verifier and takes seats **deterministically**: against five honest verifiers, 18 hashes secured 2 of 3 seats and minted half the genesis supply for a bond cost of 200. The cost is linear in registry size, hence effectively free, and **an honest beacon does not help** — the attack needs late registration, not a manipulated seed. The most important observation: **all ten invariants hold under this attack.** The ledger obeyed its own derivation rule exactly, and invariants measure compliance rather than the rule's soundness — the invariant family is structurally blind to a defect in the rule itself (lesson L5). The hypergeometric table is therefore a target under the assumption that the registry closes before the seed is public, not an achieved defense, and the **eligibility cutoff** that would enforce it (fixing eligibility before the randomness is knowable, which needs an epoch ordering the ledger lacks) is the next slice. The attack ships as the `sortition_id_grinding` scenario so the number is published, with tests pinning both its success and the invariant suite's blindness so a future cutoff breaks them loudly.
- **The next slice (E) made that cutoff a ledger rule — and its own review then enlarged the residue again.** An epoch is opened by an authority-signed transaction carrying the seed, and the eligible set is the **ancestor closure** of that opening (∩ confirmed). Ancestors are committed through the parents into the tx-id, so nothing enters an opening's past once it exists — whereas fold order can be moved by grinding `topo_order`'s min-tx-id tie-break, which is why it could never ground eligibility. **The same 18-hash grind now buys 0 seats** and the solo issuance is rejected. The invariants gained sight here: eligibility is recomputed from the raw DAG ancestry, so neutering the rule fires `committee_membership` 100/100 (with `attestation_bound` co-firing), and a new eleventh invariant `epoch_opened` fires 100/100 when the opening requirement is removed and **alone** in 63/100 when the `duplicate epoch` guard is dropped. A review also measured and fixed that without binding the parents into the opening's signature, anyone could copy it and steal the whole eligible set. **But the final pre-merge review enlarged the residue again:** (1) issuance single-use is per **(epoch, supplier)**, not per epoch, and supplier is unauthenticated — so a committee capturing one epoch mints **without limit, and indefinitely since epochs never expire**: twenty issuances took supply from 100,000 to 20,100,000 while **all eleven invariants held**. The hypergeometric table therefore prices the **probability of capture, not the loss given capture.** (2) Censorship is not the authority's alone — a third party can invalidate the transaction an opening hangs from with one transfer and erase the epoch, after which recovery needs the authority's key. Both ship as scenarios. Outstanding: a per-epoch issuance cap with expiry, and beacon-on-ledger so the authority cannot choose the seed.
- **The next slice (P) made the loss finite with a cap — and the place it declared fixed was breached again by the final review.** Genesis seeds a per-epoch cumulative ceiling, the epoch record accumulates confirmed issuance, and the gate sits after signature verification and before any state change, so an over-cap attempt burns neither the authorization nor a nonce. **The same twenty-issuance attack now halts at the ceiling** (5 confirmed; supply 100,000 → 105,000). A twelfth invariant sums confirmed issuance per epoch directly from the raw DAG — reading the ledger's own bookkeeping back would be a mirror — and fires **100/100 and alone** when the ceiling is raised, in contrast to the co-firing seen in every S and E teeth run. **But the review found the same defect twice, at two different depths.** Opening keys on `epoch_authority`, not `committee_size`, so an "authority but no sortition" genesis created epochs with no cap at all and confirmed 999,999 against a supply of 10,000. That configuration was fixed to **demand** a cap — and in that mode nothing required the epoch to *exist*, so **an issuance naming a never-opened epoch id skipped the gate entirely**: slice E's attack reproduced in full in front of a live cap (20/20 confirmed, supply two-hundredfold, the epoch table empty). Epoch existence is now a precondition wherever a cap is configured, and invariant 11 reads the genesis authority instead of `committee_size`, so that mode is no longer a blind spot (lesson L7).
- **What the cap does not bound is recorded alongside it**: the cap bounds **one epoch**, not the ledger — the authority decides how many epochs exist, and 10 epochs × 5,000 = 50,000 was minted with all twelve invariants holding. The ceiling is a policy constant, not measured verification work, and its value lives in an unauthenticated, min-tx-id-selected genesis field (a genesis carrying a ceiling of 10¹⁸ won on a handful of grinds). And the cap makes epoch quota a first-come-first-served resource, so which of two honest suppliers is evicted is decided by that same movable tie-break — the mutual eviction slice W removed by putting the supplier in the replay key, returning on the amount axis. The hypergeometric table therefore now prices the expected loss of **one epoch**.
- **The next slice (A2) established ownership — and this slice's largest result is the fact that the defect went unpublished for ten slices.** `apply_tx` checked only `balances[tx.sender] >= amount`. A third party holding no keys built a transaction in the victim's name, **took the entire 100,000, and it confirmed — with all twelve invariants silent**, because the ledger obeyed its own rules and the rules had no ownership in them. The fact appeared in none of `ledger/README`'s limits, none of this summary, none of the whitepaper, none of the report. One limit mentions the missing sender authentication, but in the context of *registry authenticity*, which hid the larger fact behind it. An account address is now the first 128 bits of `sha256("AXNADDR" ‖ pubkey)` — **deliberately wider** than the 64-bit tx-id, because a tx-id collision is an accident and an address collision is a theft — constructors take a **private key** rather than a sender string, so building a transaction in someone else's name means calling a function named `forge`, and the authentication gate runs **ahead of** the nonce check on every folding path.
- **But the substance of this slice is that it ran four adversarial reviews, and all four returned the same diagnosis: the named instance was fixed and the rule was not.** Round 1 found tx-id slot squatting — a regression this slice **opened**, since putting `auth` outside the tx-id let an unsigned copy squat an honest transaction's slot. Closing it, round 2 found the same attack on the **epoch-id slot** (a third party copies the authority's signature into its own opening: **6,724 of 8,024 confirmed transactions dropped, 100/100 seeds, all thirteen invariants silent**). Round 2 applied "invalid input is a reason, not an exception" to two fields; round 3 found the other **ten** (one transaction from a stranger with no balance killed **200/200 seeds across both sweeps** with **zero invariants firing** — `confirm()` dies, so the invariants never run). Round 3 was answered with a field-shape table; round 4 found **`tx_type`, the table's own index**. That last one is the sharpest: **an adversary generated from a table can never test that table's index**, because its value space is the table's image — so all five omission locks were blind in the same direction. The four reviews found **22 defects** between them and **16 fix commits** answered. Also closed along the way: a third party settling someone else's escrow, a duplicate verifier-key registration that made one signature count twice (supply 100,000 → 150,000), a forged genesis that killed the whole fold, and a slash authorization replayed under another name.
- **What is not closed ships as measured scenarios, not prose.** Addresses are free to mint, so replay slots are still unbounded in count (the cap bounds that, not authentication). The opening authority still buys 2 of 3 seats for 18 hashes. A channel id is burned permanently at **zero net cost**. An admissible forged genesis still takes the selection. And the most structural one — **all thirteen invariants measure only over-admission.** Under-admission, an honest transaction being *dropped*, has nine enumerated mechanisms and **not one row where any invariant fires** (lesson L8).
- **The next slice (B2L) removed the epoch-opening authority — and its conclusion is not a success but three honest records.** The actor who opened epochs is gone. `epoch_tx` and the genesis `epoch_authority` are deleted, replaced by **commit–reveal rounds** published by registered verifiers. A round **seals** when its confirmed reveal set equals its confirmed commit set, and the seed is the **content** of that set rather than anyone's choice. The eligibility cutoff moved forward to the **commit phase** — at commit time no envelope is open — and the round closes **structurally**, using neither a clock, nor a quorum adjudicator, nor fold order: a commit whose ancestry holds a reveal of that round is rejected. A fourteenth invariant, `beacon_sound`, re-derives each confirmed epoch's seed **from the raw DAG's reveals** and fires 100/100 alone under three separate mutations.
- **But the eligibility cutoff slice E published as closed survived neither candidate rule.** Under the shipped rule (eligibility = union of the commits' ancestries) an adversary stakes two addresses on a side branch and runs a **private round** — eligibility is its own two, the draw degenerates, both take seats, and **50,000 is minted for 200 in bonds with zero grinding.** Round ids are free strings, so **twenty rounds reach a supply of 20,100,000** — bit-for-bit the number this repository cites as the pre-cap disaster. The cap bounds an **epoch**, not an attacker. The alternative rule (eligibility = the registry at seal time) was implemented and measured: the private round dies, but the **last revealer computes the seed and then registers**. Both cost about 200 in bonds.
- **And the reason given for rejecting that alternative was false.** It was rejected because it "reopens what slice E closed" — and the pre-merge review measured that the same hole is open under the **shipped** rule too. That scenario had built the entire attack and simply never had the adversary **commit to the round**; adding two commits on the same side branch takes eligibility 5 → 7, seats 0 → 2 of 3, the solo mint from rejected to confirmed, and supply to 150,000. The justification is withdrawn, and the honest reason for keeping rule A is now recorded: its eligibility is tx-id-bound ancestry, which an invariant can re-derive locally (which is why invariant 10 has 100/100 teeth), whereas the alternative rests on fold order and moves a hole of the same size somewhere the invariants see it less well. **Neither closes the cutoff; what is missing is a clock.** That, and both rules' measurements, are recorded in the report and the spec — keeping a rejected design together with its numbers is this repository's discipline (lesson L9).
- **The methodological lesson this slice left (L6)**: three of the four checks meant to verify the eligibility rule **passed with the rule deleted** — in a linear chain, ancestry and fold order give the same answer. To measure rule A, build the case where rule B cannot produce the same answer (fork the late stake onto a sibling branch and grind its tx-id so it folds *first* yet stays outside the opening's past).
- Two limits survive both slices: registry authenticity rests on an unauthenticated genesis and unauthenticated stake transactions (transaction sender-signature authentication is out of scope, so any funded account id can register, and registration is first-come, permitting an honest verifier's id to be squatted); and the ledger cannot check that the signed amount equals the work actually verified — only the prototype-level invariant `issuance_anchored` (136/200) spans that gap.
- **The next slice (CLK) gave rounds a monotone number — and the headline is not "capture is closed" but "the eligibility axis is closed and the seed axis is measured and published"; what this slice bought is one preparatory round.** Round and epoch identifiers went from free strings to **monotone integers**. `beacon_commit` / `beacon_reveal` carry a `round_index: int` that must equal `state.next_round` **exactly** (reason `round index is not the next round`), and only a seal increments `next_round`. The old reason `round already sealed` is **absorbed** into that one line and the new gate is **wider** — it blocks not only rejoining the past but **claiming the future**. And eligibility moved one round forward: eligible(n) = the **union of the ancestries of round n−1's confirmed reveals** ∪ the genesis registry, with eligible(0) = the genesis registry — so eligibility freezes **before** round n's commits exist. **That tense is the point, and the number is what buys it** — it is exactly what rule B, which B2L rejected, did not have (under B the last revealer computed the seed and then registered, buying seats for 46 hashes). The behavioral change ships with it: a verifier that registers by stake becomes drawable **one round later**. An optional genesis field `epoch_expiry_window` and the reason `epoch issuance window expired` arrived too (gate order: `insufficient valid signatures` → `epoch not opened` → **expiry** → `epoch issuance cap exceeded`), and **unlike the cap it is not declared mandatory** — an epoch without a cap is an unbounded loss, whereas an epoch without an expiry is a hygiene problem.
- **The invariants went from fourteen to fifteen, and the new fifteenth sees the other half of B2L's pre-merge review finding F4.** What F4 measured was: "no invariant guards the rule that keeps a seal final — invariant 12 catches an `issued` rewind, but **nobody watches the half where the seed and the eligibility diverge**." The new `round_sequence_sound` **re-derives** from the raw DAG that ① the set of confirmed epoch numbers == {0…K−1} and ② each n's eligibility == the ancestries of round n−1's confirmed reveals ∪ the genesis registry, reading neither `state.next_round` nor `pending_eligible` nor `epochs[n]["eligible"]` and never calling `eligible_verifiers`. In a teeth run that **causes a real re-seal**, only the fifteenth fires, **alone, 100/100**, while `beacon_sound` stays silent — the latter re-derives the seed from the final confirmed set, so once the re-seal settles the gate and the oracle agree again. Closed alongside it: **the path that privatizes eligibility with a single private round** and **opening R rounds at once**, plus four axes of §8 ("an invalid input is a rejection reason, not an exception") — the magnitude of an integer (`MAX_INT_DIGITS = 640`), the depth of a structure (`MAX_CONTAINER_DEPTH = 4`), the cost of a traversal (`MAX_TRAVERSAL_STEPS = 1024`), and rendered bytes (`MAX_RENDER_BYTES = 524_288`).
- **The price list ships as numbers, not prose.** One preparatory round = **4 transactions · 0 grinds · 0 additional bond**. Pay it and eligibility goes 0 → 2, seats 0/3 → **2/3**, supply 100,000 → **150,000**. **The seed axis does not close** — an early sealer becomes that round's **only revealer**, so even with eligibility spanning all **7** registrants, **11 salts** buy 2 of 3 seats. **The number does not forbid repetition; it serializes it**: at a cap of 1,000,000 with R=20 the supply reaches **20,100,000** (bit-for-bit B2L's number) at a cost of 4 transactions per round. **One grind of the genesis registry** skips the preparatory round in **0 rounds** (0 bond, 6 transactions, 2/3 seats, supply 150,000). And **an early seal is capture and blanket censorship at once** — even with all 5 honest verifiers committing, eligible(1) = the adversary's 2, so **all 5 honest stakers are excluded from the next epoch's committee**.
- **The worst thing this slice newly opened is an escalation in the halting grade.** Withhold one reveal and the old rule stalled only **one named round**; now that the number serializes rounds, **the whole ledger** stops. **Recovery paths 1 → 0.** Worse, **the honest side's only available action** — a verifier that has not yet committed joining in — grows the commit set 3 → 5 and pushes the **sealing condition further away**. **The expiry window has no invariant** — it depends on fold-time state (`next_round`), and imitating it through ancestry lets the issuer pick parents to neuter it. Making it a primary fact via an `at_round` field was rejected at design time (an adversary that under-reports and keeps its ancestry thin makes it silent, so a partial invariant becomes a value that only pretends to exist). The **tenth** §8 axis — the size of the string an adversarial `__repr__` returns — would require changing `_content_repr`'s explicit contract (tx-id stability) to close, so it **ships as a design decision**.
- **Three rounds of adversarial review each returned a Critical, and all three were §8** — the magnitude of a value → the cost of a traversal → rendered bytes. All three arose **on top of a green suite**, and all three **satisfied exactly** every rule in force at the time. Just as an adversary grown from a table cannot measure that table's own index (lesson L12), a lock that closes one axis is blind to the axis beside it.

- **The next slice (SLH) turned that 0 into a 1 — the headline is "withholding went from free to two bonds," and the subtitle is "forbidding self-forfeiture is a price, not a barrier."** A ninth transaction type arrived: `reveal_default(round_index, defaulter, witness)`. A **witness** is a transaction the defaulter signed and got confirmed whose ancestry already holds a confirmed reveal of that round — it proves liveness and non-cooperation **at once**, and **a slasher cannot build one**. There are six gates; only the first (`witness does not prove selective absence`) reads ancestry and therefore lives in **`confirm`**, while the other five (`round index is not the next round` · `not a registered verifier` · `sender is the defaulter` · `not a committer of this round` · `already revealed for this round`) live in `apply_tx`. The effect is `tax_pool += stakes.pop(defaulter, 0)` · `verifiers.pop(defaulter)` · `del beacon_commits[(round, defaulter)]`, so the commit set shrinks, `committed == revealed` holds again, and the round **seals**. A sixteenth invariant, `bond_forfeiture_sound`, fires **100/100** on teeth 16a/16b (the conservation invariants stay silent — evidence it is not a restatement of invariant 1) and **0/100** on two mirror mutants. **Recovery paths 0 → 1**: one forfeiture takes commits 3 → 2, epochs `[0,1]`, `next_round` 1 → 2, a confirmed issuance of 500, and supply 100,000 → **100,500**. The old values are kept as history — the round never sealed, `next_round` stalled forever, zero confirmed issuance, recovery **0**.
- **The whole price list is measured and published, and its first line is "this slice opened no new axis; it priced an axis already open."** The g-collusion curve fits the **same closed form** as `prepared_commit_grinding` — at distinct seed counts `[1,2,4,8]` the capture rates are **0.133 · 0.245 · 0.448 · 0.657** (hypergeometric baseline 0.143). That path is unlimited and free and reached a capture rate of **1.0000**, whereas this one stops at 0.657, so the SLH path is **dominated by the free path already open and does not enlarge the adversary's capability.** **One bit of the seed costs two bonds** — two seeds differ in **31 of 32 bytes** (`4b30cb85…` vs `c1438764…`), seats go **1/3 → 2/3**, an issuance of 50,000 goes from rejected to confirmed, and supply 100,000 → **150,000**. The price is 100 forfeited + 100 locked across 4 transactions, and the locked bond is unrecoverable because the nine `FIELD_SHAPES` entries contain **zero `unstake`**. **Forbidding self-forfeiture is a price, not a barrier** — the detour was measured **before** the gate was added, and a defaulter that registers a second address to forfeit itself buys **the same one bit** (arm W2 does not differ from the self-forfeit arm **in a single value**). The price merely went from 1 to **2**.

If it becomes the next round's defaulter, the locked bond becomes the next burn, so **the marginal cost converges to one bond per bit** — derived by reading the rules, not measured.
- **Total silence cannot be slashed.** A party that neither reveals nor transacts leaves no witness that **can exist** — we fed **all 26** confirmed transactions in as witness candidates and measured **0 passing**. Telling that apart from a crash needs a real clock, and that is outside this slice.
- **The slasher has no incentive — this rule presumes altruistic participants.** The change in (balance + stake) across all 5 registered verifiers is **0**. The forfeited bond goes to `tax_pool` and no rule reduces it. The **dead seat** ships alongside: seats freeze at the seal and forfeiture comes after, so a forfeited party keeps its seat and loses only the ability to sign — in e2e the old signer policy drew it and **the honest spine issued nothing** (supply 10,000 unchanged; drawing from live seats gives **10,064**). The policy was fixed, but that only **defers it by one**: a C=3, k=2 configuration survives exactly **one** forfeiture.

- **The next slice (TIME) put a time surrogate at the commit stage — the headline is not "an axis was closed" but "the quorum `q` does not close an axis, it prices one — and the crossing point `q=3` we first published is withdrawn."** A genesis field `seed_quorum` (=`q`, default **1**) arrived and the seal forked in two. `S(n) = { commit c : at least q of this round's confirmed reveals have c in their ancestry }`, and if `|S| >= q` the seal is **normal** (the seed is S's reveals, and a committee is drawn); otherwise it is **degraded** (`seed = None`, committee `None` — yet the epoch is still created and `next_round` still increments). The count is written into `state.seed_support` by `confirm` as it folds a reveal and rolled back if the reveal is rejected, and it attaches **to the commit currently registered, not to the sender**. What decides whether a commit enters the seed is no longer "what does it have in its ancestry" but **"what has it in *their* ancestry"** — the one direction an adversary cannot forge. **No new transaction type**: one genesis field.
- **Three axes ship in one table, and that table is this slice's conclusion** (measured at N=5, C=3, k=2). ① **Buying the bias** (the late envelope) — threshold `m >= q`, price **0 forfeited + 100 x q locked**, 0 rejections, **0 of 16** invariants firing; raising `q` makes it **more expensive** and there is nothing to undo. ② **Withholding** — threshold `w* = N - q + 1`, price 100 x w forfeited + **w registrations lost**; raising `q` makes it **cheaper**, and forfeiture restores **only the seal**. ③ **The detached reveal** — threshold `k* = N - q + 1`, price **0**, 0 rejections, **0 of 16** invariants firing; raising `q` makes it **cheaper** and there is **no** undo. **Why the recommendation is withdrawn**: the crossing put ①'s **bond count** and ②'s **head count** on one axis, and the two are **not the same unit** — and ③ has **the same threshold as ② at a price of zero** with no undo, so the availability side of the trade **can be bought for free and there is nothing left to trade.** Raising `q` makes one priced axis more expensive and **one free axis cheaper.** ⚠️ **What is withdrawn is the recommendation, not the observation** — "there is one cell where the two numbers coincide" is a measurement and is not deleted; only the reading that says *pick that cell* changes. The main-line judgment `q=1` (the rule off by default) is **more strongly** supported by the third axis, not less.
- **And `q`'s teeth are causal, not economic.** One unit of support costs one bond only because the adversarial commit sits in the honest reveals' **future**; the same commit sitting **inside their past** takes support to 6 on a single bond and passes all the way to `q=5`. A late commit cannot enter that past **at any price**. The correct reading is not "`q` bonds buy it" but **"buying the seed *in the honest reveals' future* costs `q` bonds."** ⚠️ **Salt grinding works on that arm only because the simulator makes honest secrets deterministic and exposes them** — in a real system that commit would have to be built blind, so on arms where several revealers mix, the published salt counts are **optimistic**.

- **One thing proved by measurement to be unguardable, and one thing shipped as an unclosed limit.** The first — `q > the committer count` is a **dead ledger** (every sealed round degraded, **0 rejections**, issuance permanently `epoch not opened`), yet the sortition genesis registry is **empty** and `n` is a fold-time value, so the genesis cannot decide it.
